# Felt sphere

Open `felt_sphere.usda` with the usdGen plugins loaded. Use camera `/Camera`
and Storm or **Moonray (debug)**, with scene materials and scene lights enabled.

The 24 cm diameter sphere is a welded all-quad Catmull–Clark mesh. Three live
usdGen descriptions copy the current Puppet A yellow felt: dense nap, raised
curl clumps, and fine strays. Scattering evaluates the level-3 limit surface.
Ptex controls and surface textures are baked for this sphere's face topology;
all runtime texture dependencies are relative files in `textures/`.

The example uses 1.65 million nap roots/m², 450,000 curl roots/m² and 7,000
strays/m², with the puppet's physical fibre lengths and widths. The sphere
uses looser clumping: Ptex tightness multipliers are 0.18 for nap and 0.45 for
raised curls, with softer root-to-tip profiles and 25% partial clump escape.
This spreads existing fibres between bundles without increasing root density.
It has no facial holdouts and no authored PointInstancer or
BasisCurves replacement groom.

The 100-frame animation demos now live in [examples/motion](../motion/README.md).

## Three-point studio

The scene contains three standard UsdLux RectLights under `/Studio`:

| Light | Position / purpose | Size (m) | Intensity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Key | Upper camera-left; broad modelling light | 1.5 × 1.5 | 12 |
| Fill | Camera-right; weaker, slightly cool fill | 2 × 2 | 1.7 |
| Back | Behind and to the right; fibre edge separation | 1 × 1.8 | 16 |

Exposure is zero and normalization is off for all three. The key is slightly
warm; the back light is neutral. `/Studio/Environment` adds a contrasting
HDR dome at intensity 0.12. `textures/studio_contrast.exr` is a linear, tiled,
mipmapped lat-long EXR with bright softbox windows and dark walls (values from
about 0.012 to 16), generated by `make_studio_hdr.py`. Its restrained intensity
preserves the shaping from the key, fill and back light. The light
transforms aim their local -Z emission axes at the sphere. The two felt motion
scenes use the same fixed studio under `/World/Studio`, centered at Z=0.5;
the lights do not move with the animated sphere.

In usdview, enable **Scene Lights** and **Scene Materials**, disable the camera
light and the **extra viewer dome**, select `/Camera`, and use Very High
complexity for Storm. The scene's HDR dome remains enabled by Scene Lights.
Choose **Moonray (debug)** for the in-process Windows renderer; the separate
Arras-backed Moonray delegate cannot start local sessions in this build.
Storm approximates area lights as point sources; Moonray resolves their finite
area and soft shadows, so their images are not expected to match pixel for pixel.

`studio_lighting.py` updates all three felt scenes and supplies the same light
authoring function to their generators. `check_studio.py` runs through
testusdview and captures both renderers with scene lighting only, then disables
each light separately to verify its visible contribution. Results and comparison
images are in `studio_validation/felt_sphere/` (and beside the motion scenes).

## Moonray felt preset

The stage selects `/Render/FeltMoonray` through `renderSettingsPrimPath`.
`configure_felt_sphere.py` authors both the open clumping and this preset; the
asset generator reapplies them when rebuilding the sphere.

The preset uses adaptive sampling (32 minimum, 128 maximum primary samples,
target error 3), one light and one BSDF sample per primary hit, four total
bounces and two diffuse/glossy bounces. Motion blur and depth of field are off
for this static study. Primary sampling is prioritized to resolve the fine
fibres instead of multiplying secondary rays at each primary hit.

This requires the updated local hdMoonray build: it now consumes the active
UsdRenderSettings prim and its `moonray:sceneVariable:*` attributes. Restart
usdview after rebuilding the delegate. Scene setting edits are live, and
removing/deactivating the settings restores the host/default configuration.
The viewport still controls framing and output resolution; this does not add
offline render-product export. Storm ignores the Moonray-specific attributes.

`check_groom.py` verifies live setting readback, edits and removal, captures
the new groom, and compares the preset with default sampling and a 256-sample
uniform reference. Its results are in `groom_validation/`.

At 640 × 640 on the local machine, the HDR-lit preset converged in 20.1 seconds
versus 26.4 seconds for default sampling. Mean RGB error against the 256-sample
reference was 0.80 versus 0.70 on the 0–255 image scale. This is a faster lookdev
tradeoff, not a claim of lower noise; increase the adaptive ceiling for finals.
